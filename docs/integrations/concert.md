# IBM Concert

CALM Forge consumes Concert risk assessments as a policy input and emits posture-shaped artifacts that Concert can ingest. On the consume side, the Concert client — live when credentials are configured, fixture-backed for CI and local development — calls Concert's risk APIs to fetch the current risk posture for each workload in the knowledge graph. Those risk decisions are written into the graph as policy nodes, and the drift evaluator treats them as live policy: when Concert flags a workload, the reconciliation loop can update the workload's allowed regions or capability ceiling without a manual interview cycle. The risk decision becomes a placement constraint; the constraint becomes a typed predicate; the predicate becomes a violation gate on the next compile.

On the emit side, CALM Forge produces the three payloads Concert needs to render posture in its operator console: signed OPA policy bundles compiled from the graph predicates, compliance posture (controls and evidence derived from the workload's compliance scope and `manifests_as` edges), and the topology graph as serialized nodes and edges. Concert's low-code/no-code operator surface remains the UX layer; CALM Forge is the upstream source of typed posture data that Concert renders, observes, and reports against. The bundles are LPA-signed, so Concert can verify provenance and tamper-evidence before ingestion. The same compilation pass that produces Terraform HCL produces the Concert-shaped artifacts.

Concert remains the posture management and compliance reporting authority. CALM Forge does not replace Concert's observation, scoring, or reporting model. It compiles intent into Concert-consumable artifacts upstream and reads Concert risk decisions back into the graph downstream — closing the loop between architectural intent and operational posture.
